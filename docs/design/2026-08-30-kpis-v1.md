# KPIs V1 — agreed product design

**Status:** agreed design; tickets 01–04 implemented. Restock planning is temporarily hidden in the KPI interface while its implementation remains intact.
**Audience:** authenticated Serenity Hue staff
**Placement:** a new **KPIs** sidebar destination directly below Overview

## Intent

KPIs is a central, decision-oriented business-performance view. It is not a generic
analytics dashboard and it does not replace the live operational Overview. Its job is
to answer: how are sales performing, which products and channels drive that performance,
who is buying, and which physical variants need a purchasing decision soon.

It stays serverless on Vercel. Next.js server-side reads and repository aggregate
queries over Turso supply the page; no warehouse, separate analytics backend, event
pipeline, or client-side tracking product is introduced.

## Approved visual direction

The approved implementation reference is the Quiet Control Room KPI concept below. It
extends the current Serenity Hue Operations visual system rather than introducing a
generic analytics-dashboard language.

- [Desktop KPI concept](./kpis-v1-desktop-approved-concept.png): persistent plum
  navigation; an editorial page title and compact period/freshness controls; one
  restrained headline strip; then a sales trend, dense product/channel tables,
  customers, and Restock planning in a practical reading order.
- [Mobile KPI concept](./kpis-v1-mobile-approved-concept.png): a plum top bar with one
  menu control; touch-friendly summary values and trend; then Restock planning as the
  first action-heavy list. It has no bottom navigation and does not turn the experience

The screenshots are compositional and interaction references, not literal data or
pixel-perfect assets. Every visible amount, date, customer, and product is synthetic.
Implementation must preserve the approved hierarchy, paper-first surfaces, fine blush
rules, restrained plum/magenta treatment, dense operational tables, and explicit
action states while rendering only truthful connected data.

## Scope

### In V1

1. Global reporting-period control: 7, 30, or 90 days; **All time**; or any valid
   custom inclusive range. All time begins with the earliest recorded order and includes
   the current in-progress business day.
2. Four headline KPIs with comparison to the immediately preceding equal-length period:
   net merchandise sales, paid order count, AOV, and net units sold.
3. A daily trend chart, toggled between net merchandise sales and order count.
4. Top ten physical products, ranked by net units sold, with net product revenue and a
   Shopify/TikTok split. Physical variants roll into their parent physical product;
   safely unmapped order lines remain visibly unassigned and are never guessed.
5. A compact Shopify-versus-TikTok comparison: net merchandise sales, order count,
   AOV, and share of net units.
6. Customer KPIs: new versus repeat active customers and the top five customers by net
   spend, order count, and latest purchase date.
7. Restock planning: an action queue based on real physical-variant demand, shown under
   the parent product for readability. It is implemented but temporarily hidden from
   the KPI interface.
8. A quiet freshness timestamp based on the latest completed source sync. The page
   refreshes automatically after imports or relevant edits through serverless cache
   invalidation; staff do not manually refresh it.

### Explicitly deferred

- Contextual analytics on product, customer, and order detail pages.
- Customer detail pages and chatbot-style analysis.
- CSV/export workflows.
- Finance/reconciliation analytics for VAT/tax and delivery amounts.
- Reorder quantity recommendations.
- Email, push, and in-app KPI/restock alerts.
- Role-based KPI access controls (all current app staff may access KPIs).
- A separate analytics infrastructure, data warehouse, or third-party tracking service.

## Shared reporting rules

- **Business timezone:** Europe/London. It determines chart days, filter boundaries,
  comparisons, and forecast dates—not the viewer's browser timezone.
- **All-time trend labels:** x-axis labels include the calendar year (for example,
  `Apr 2025`) so repeated months are never ambiguous; the hover label includes the
  complete date.
- **Net merchandise sales:** product-line revenue after discounts and refunds. VAT/tax
  and delivery charges are excluded entirely from V1 KPIs.
- **Refund timing:** a refund adjusts revenue and units on the date it is issued, not by
  rewriting the earlier purchase day.
- **Eligible sale:** a paid, non-cancelled transaction/line. Refunds reduce its revenue
  and units. The implementation must support partial refunds where the source data
  identifies them.
- **Order count:** an operational paid, non-cancelled order count, reported separately
  from net sales.
- **AOV:** net merchandise sales divided by the reported paid, non-cancelled order
  count; KPI labels/tooltips must make this definition clear.
- **Comparisons:** the fixed and custom ranges compare all four headline KPIs with the
  directly preceding interval of exactly the same calendar length. All time deliberately
  shows no synthetic previous-period comparison.
- **Data quality:** preserve unknown/unmapped source data visibly. Never infer a
  product mapping, forecast, or customer identity to make a chart look complete.

## Customer metrics

Customer counts are restricted to customers who made a qualifying purchase during the
selected reporting period. Each is classified using their full recorded qualifying
order count: one order means **new**; more than one means **repeat**. There is no
arbitrary one-month waiting rule and no cohort-boundary rule.

Customer identity is intentionally conservative:

1. An exact normalized email is the primary identity match across channels.
2. When no usable email exists, a normalized phone number plus a compatible normalized
   name may match.
3. A phone-only or name-only match never merges customers.
4. Orders that cannot be safely identified still contribute to sales and order KPIs,
   but do not contribute to customer counts.

Name normalization may remove harmless casing, whitespace, punctuation, and comparable
middle-name variation, but must not use loose fuzzy matching that could merge household
members sharing a phone number.

## Restock planning

Restock planning replaces a static low-stock/watch-list presentation. It is an action
queue, not an inventory snapshot.

### Forecast calculation

For every physical variant, calculate demand from trailing 90 calendar days of eligible
source sales, across both sales channels, independent of the page's reporting-period
filter. Refunds reduce those net units. Demand must be positive.

Use the physical variant's current counted stock and stored supplier lead time to derive:

```text
daily demand       = net eligible units over trailing 90 days / 90
forecast stockout  = today + current counted stock / daily demand
reorder-by date    = forecast stockout - supplier lead time - 7 calendar-day safety buffer
```

The UI may present the parent physical product as the row, but the calculations and
urgency ordering remain physical-variant specific. A row must identify the affected
variant(s) rather than implying that all variants are at risk.

### Eligibility and visibility

A physical variant appears only when all of the following are true:

- it has a current counted stock level;
- it has a stored supplier lead time;
- at least 30 days have elapsed since its first recorded paid sale;
- its trailing-90-day net demand is positive; and
- it has a defensible mapped relationship between sales line and physical variant.

Do not manufacture forecast dates. Items missing any input, with insufficient history,
or with zero/negative demand do not appear in V1—not even in a separate data-quality
list.

Show only variants whose reorder-by date is overdue or occurs within the coming 30
days, ordered by urgency. Each row shows current counted stock, forecast stockout date,
and reorder-by date (including an overdue state when applicable). V1 intentionally
does not recommend a purchase quantity, because minimum order quantities, pack sizes,
target cover, and purchasing cadence are not yet reliable system inputs.

## Presentation order

1. KPIs heading, period control, and data-current timestamp.
2. Headline KPI strip.
3. Sales trend.
4. Top products and channel performance.
5. Customer KPIs and top customers.
6. Restock planning is temporarily hidden.

Use the established warm-paper, plum/magenta, editorial operations design language.
Avoid a generic dashboard card wall, decorative charts, pie charts, duplicate stock
snapshots, and faux precision. The comparison table is preferable to a pie chart: it
helps staff inspect Shopify and TikTok without implying that their deliberately
independent channel stock is one shared source.

## Implementation constraints to preserve

- All data access stays behind repository/domain interfaces; pages and route adapters
  issue no SQL.
- No client-side analytics SDK or external user-behaviour tracking is introduced.
- Aggregate queries are scoped to the selected reporting range and the fixed 90-day
  restock window. Custom ranges have no artificial duration limit; All time resolves to
  the complete recorded order history.
- Cache tags/revalidation must occur after successful imports and relevant order,
  refund, physical-inventory, mapping, or lead-time edits.
- Existing physical inventory, channel listing, and Lab/packaging separations remain
  intact.
- Use visible zero, empty, and unavailable states instead of misleading averages or
  placeholder estimates.

KPIs, explained like you’re 5

The KPI page is a scoreboard for the shop. It answers three simple questions: “How much did we sell?”, “What caused it?”, and “Where should we look next?”

Important: an Offer review is a helpful “look here first” label. It is not a promise, an automatic discount, a commission decision, or a message sent to anyone.

First: what is Offer review?

Imagine three sticky notes on creator names. The app looks at the creator’s attributed sales, orders, activity, and rank during the selected comparison window. Then it places one sticky note beside them.

🌟
Priority review

“This creator has stronger evidence. Look at them first.”

What earns it?

At least 3 attributed orders, activity in at least 2 weeks or months, and a top-five position by revenue or orders.

👀
Watch

“Something is happening, but we need more proof.”

What earns it?

The creator has some positive sales, orders, units, or activity, but not enough to be a priority yet.

⏳
Needs more activity

“We do not have enough recent evidence to judge.”

What does it mean?

No positive attributed activity appeared in the selected comparison periods. It does not mean the creator is bad.

So why was it added?

Because a long creator table gives you numbers, but numbers alone do not tell you who deserves attention first. Offer review turns those numbers into a small, explainable shortlist signal.

It tells you: “Based on the evidence we have right now, how urgently should I inspect this creator?”

It does not tell you: profit margin, content quality, audience fit, follower quality, commission cost, stock availability, or whether you must send an offer.

The whole KPI page in one picture

The page is split into focused rooms. Each room answers a different business question.

1

Controls

Choose the date window. The page uses Europe/London calendar dates and GBP.

2

Overview

The big scoreboard: sales, orders, average order value, units, trends, channels, products, and customers.

3

Products

Find what sold, where it sold, and which products are winning over time.

4

Channels

Compare Shopify and TikTok Shop without mixing their numbers together.

5

Customers

See new versus repeat customers and the customers behind the period’s sales.

6

TikTok Affiliates

Understand creator-attributed sales, videos, products, commissions, and offer-review signals.

1. Date controls

🗓️

7 days, 30 days, 90 days, All time, Custom

These buttons tell the scoreboard which slice of time to use. “All time” means all retained/recorded history available to the app.

🔍

Comparison and freshness

The page can compare the selected window with the preceding window. It also tells you when the data was last updated and whether a source is unavailable.

2. Overview tab

This is the “how is the shop doing?” page.

The four big numbers

💷
Net salesMoney from eligible orders, minus processed refunds.
🧾
OrdersHow many eligible orders were counted.
➗
Average order value (AOV)Net sales ÷ orders. If there are no orders, it stays safely at zero.
📦
Net unitsUnits sold minus refunded units.

Net sales trend

Each point is a day’s sales. The line helps you see peaks and quiet days.

Hover or focus a point: see the exact date and value. Open “View chart data” when you want the exact list without guessing from the line.

🍰

Channel mix

A split of paid orders between Shopify and TikTok Shop. It answers, “Where did the orders come from?”

The mix uses order-count logic so a channel with a negative revenue adjustment does not make the picture confusing.

🏆

Product performance

Products are ranked by net units, while net revenue is also shown. This answers, “What did people buy?”

The table keeps product names and their values in aligned columns so each number sits under its own heading.

👥

Customer composition

New means one recorded order. Repeat means more than one order across the recorded history.

This is based on the history the app knows about; it does not pretend to know missing history.

📣

TikTok Ads spend

Ad spend is shown as a separate strip because advertising cost is not shop revenue. If TikTok Ads is unavailable, the page says so instead of inventing a number.

3. Products tab

This is the “which products are winning?” room.

🛍️

Summary, weekly, monthly

Summary shows the selected date range. Weekly compares the latest four Monday–Sunday weeks. Monthly compares the latest calendar months.

The latest partial week or month is labelled. When needed, it compares the same number of elapsed days for a fairer comparison.

🔀

Best seller by

Switch the winner rule between Net units and Net revenue. That lets you find the product that moved the most or made the most money.

4. Channels tab

This is the “Shopify versus TikTok Shop” room. They stay separate because they are different selling channels.

🟣

Each channel card

💷
Total revenueNet revenue attributed to that channel.
🧾
OrdersOrders from that channel.
➗
AOVRevenue divided by channel orders.
📦
Net unitsUnits sold minus refunded units for that channel.
📊
Unit shareThat channel’s share of the counted units.
🎯

Why the alignment matters

The heading and the number are one little pair. They should sit together and have the same visual anchor.

The cards are now balanced and centred, while the metric label and its value are consistently aligned. This prevents “AOV” from looking like it belongs to a different number.

Channel quantities can legitimately differ. The page does not force Shopify and TikTok Shop to add up in a misleading way.

5. Customers tab

🌱

New versus repeat

New customers have one known order. Repeat customers have more than one known order. This helps answer, “Are people coming back?”

📇

Customer table

For each known customer, the table can show period net spend, order count, and latest purchase. Matching stays conservative so the app does not accidentally merge two different people.

6. TikTok Affiliates tab

This is the “which creators helped sell?” room. Affiliate numbers are kept separate from ordinary shop totals because one order can appear in both views.

The six headline numbers

💷
Affiliate net salesAttributed TikTok revenue after linked refunds.
🤝
Estimated commissionTikTok’s estimated creator commission; it is not your revenue.
🧾
Attributed ordersOrders or order lines linked to affiliate activity, as labelled by the report.
🧑‍🎤
Active affiliatesCreators with attributed sales in the selected period.
🎬
Published videosPublished creator videos; LIVE content is excluded from this count.
🧩
Unreconciled GMVAffiliate sales that could not be matched to an ordinary order yet.
📈

Affiliate trend

Shows affiliate-attributed sales over time. Hover or focus a point for the exact date and value. “View chart data” gives you the exact values in a readable list.

Unreconciled GMV remains visible as its own explanation rather than being quietly added to shop revenue.

🧭

Comparison toggle

Summary gives a current-period snapshot. Weekly compares the latest four calendar weeks. Monthly compares the latest three calendar months.

The table shows creator, videos, net sales, commission, orders, units, best product, and Offer review. On small screens, creator details can be expanded instead of being squeezed into a tiny table.

🏅

Creator leaderboard

Rank creators by Revenue, Orders, or Commission. Search creators, view the first ten, or show everyone.

“Best product” means the product most associated with that creator’s attributed activity in the selected view.

📦

Products sold by affiliates

A product-level view of affiliate net sales, units, and orders. It answers, “What did creators help sell?”

📘

About this affiliate report

Explains the history window, unreconciled sales, and commission meaning, so the report’s numbers are not mistaken for the ordinary Shop totals.

7. TikTok Ads tab

📣

Advertising is a cost

This tab shows the provider-reported TikTok Ads spend and performance state. It is not shop revenue and it is not affiliate commission.

🔌

Connection status matters

The page shows whether the ads source is connected and whether the report is available. Missing data is labelled instead of being made up.

8. Restock tab

🚚

“What might run out?”

Restock uses the counted physical catalogue, recent sales history, lead time, and urgency to suggest which physical variants deserve attention.

It does not change stock. It does not change Shopify quantities, TikTok quantities, or the product catalogue. It is a decision aid.

What was added or strengthened

🖱️

Charts can be inspected

Trend charts now support hover/focus tooltips and an exact-value disclosure, so a peak is not just a pretty shape—you can read the date and amount.

📐

Tables line up

Column headings, labels, and values use the same column structure. Channel cards also keep each metric label paired with its number and balance the card layout.

🔁

Products have time views

Products can be viewed as a summary, weekly comparison, or monthly comparison, with a choice of units or revenue for the winner.

🧑‍🎤

Affiliates have time views

Affiliate performance now has Summary, Weekly, and Monthly views, with readable best-product values, creator comparisons, and the Offer review signal.

📱

Small screens are considered

Wide comparison tables can turn into expandable creator details on mobile, so important values do not get cut off.

🛡️

Data edge cases are disclosed

Refunds, missing sources, unreconciled affiliate GMV, partial periods, and unavailable ads data are handled explicitly instead of silently producing a confident-looking wrong answer.

The one-sentence version

The KPI page is a set of honest shop scoreboards: Overview tells you how the shop did, Products tells you what sold, Channels tells you where it sold, Customers tells you who bought, Affiliates tells you which creators helped, Ads tells you what promotion cost, and Restock tells you what may need attention next.

Offer review is only the app saying: “Based on recent creator activity, this name may deserve your attention first.”
